\chapter{Common health questions}
\section{Learning objectives}
Apply risk--benefit reasoning to common supplement questions; communicate uncertainty without dismissing symptoms; and identify when a question requires urgent or specialist assessment.

\section{Can vitamins provide energy?}
Calories provide energy. B vitamins are needed for pathways that release and use energy from food, but extra B vitamins do not act like a stimulant when intake is adequate. Correcting a deficiency can help with related symptoms; persistent fatigue still warrants assessment of sleep, mood, anemia, thyroid disease, infection, medication effects, and other causes. \cite{odsThiamin,odsNiacin,odsB12}

\section{Do supplements prevent chronic disease?}
The strength of evidence depends on the population, dose, comparator, duration, and outcome. A statistically significant result may be too small to matter clinically, and evidence from a food pattern should not automatically be transferred to an isolated pill. For community-dwelling, nonpregnant adults, the U.S. Preventive Services Task Force finds evidence insufficient to recommend for or against multivitamins or most single- or paired-nutrient supplements for preventing cardiovascular disease or cancer; it recommends against beta-carotene or vitamin E for that purpose. This recommendation does not apply to children, people who are pregnant or may become pregnant, people with known nutritional deficiencies, or people who are chronically ill or hospitalized. \cite{uspstfSupplements}

Some targeted supplements prevent specific deficiency-related outcomes. For example, 400 mcg of folic acid daily before and during early pregnancy can reduce neural-tube-defect risk. Supplements do not replace nutritious eating, physical activity, vaccination, or needed medical care. A supplement claim should name a dose, population, outcome, and duration. \cite{cdcFolicAcid}

\section{Is a multivitamin necessary?}
There is no universal need for a multivitamin. For people with poor intake, low appetite, or a restricted diet, it may help fill specific nutrient gaps, but it does not replace a varied diet. Compare the amounts with other products you take and, where an upper intake level has been established, consider total intake against that level. Percent Daily Value is a labeling reference, not a safety limit, and its values may differ by country. \cite{odsMVMS}

\section{What should I do with a low test result?}
Confirm what the test measures, whether the sample and units are appropriate, and whether inflammation or another illness affects interpretation. Ask what dose, duration, food changes, and follow-up are appropriate. Do not continue a high dose indefinitely simply because a number was once low.

\section{The central message}
Health comes from systems: adequate food, safe water, movement, sleep, relationships, preventive care, and treatment of disease. Vitamins and minerals are essential parts of that system, not shortcuts around it.
